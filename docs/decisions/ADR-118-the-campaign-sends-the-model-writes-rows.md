Status: accepted in part, 2026-10-24: the rules at 1.0.4.58 (Decision 1), the database at 1.0.4.59 (Decision 2), the renderer and the shadow send at 1.0.4.60 (Decision 3), the inbox side at 1.0.4.61 (Decision 4), the lead run at 1.0.4.62 (Decision 5), the card and the alert at 1.0.4.63 (Decision 6). Filled as each rung of the campaign ships: the database, the renderer and the shadow send, the inbox side, the lead run, the card, the shadow week and the switch to on. Nothing sends while this record says shadow.

## In plain words

Steve asked for an email campaign to hiring managers that runs on its own. A model is good at reading a posting and finding the one plain thing a company does, and bad at being the same twice. So the work is split down the middle. A model writes rows: who the company is, who runs its engineering team, where the address was published, one clause saying what the company makes. Code does everything that touches the send: it checks every rule, fills the approved template word for word, picks the order, holds the count, and sends. No model is in the send path, and a template changes only when Steve says yes.

This first version is the rules, written as code with no database and no mail. Each one is a test that fails the build.

## Decision

1. **The rules are code in `functions/JobHunt.Domain/Features/Campaign/`, with no I/O, each held by a test that names the review's case.**
   - `FormerEmployers`: Storee Construction, Conway Investment Research (also Conway Investment Solutions), Cass Information Systems, Hunter Engineering and the State of Missouri, with their variants and domains (storee.com, conwayis.com, cassinfo.com, hunter.com, mo.gov). Matched by name the way Rule 50 matches names, or by domain key. No date: a former employer stays out for good.
   - `CompanyKey`: the registrable domain, public-suffix aware for the suffixes this search meets (mail.acme.co.uk is acme.co.uk, dor.mo.gov is mo.gov). An unknown suffix over-joins, which over-holds.
   - `ThirtyDayRule` and `CompanyGate`: one email a company in a rolling 30 days, follow-ups counted, matched by key, by parent key, or by name. A send on day 0 holds through day 30; day 31 is the first allowed. The gate reads a former employer first and names it, then the do-not-apply list, then the 30 days.
   - `LiveThreadRule`: a tracker row for the company in the last 30 days that is a person's thread (a LinkedIn message, an email, a call, a rejection) holds the lead with its Count. An application row, the ATS sweep's receipt and an unanswered connection request do not hold: the first two are the application itself, and the third is the LinkedIn order's input.
   - `Tells` and `WhatTheyDoRule`: S1_WORDS and S1_PHRASES of ai_tells_five_signs.py in one constant, matched against a checked-in copy of the Python file by count and content. The clause opens with a verb from a list of fifteen, runs 20 to 110 characters, ends without punctuation, has no comma clause longer than the phrase before it, no dash, quote marks, braces or address, never names the company, and carries no tell. A failing clause blocks its lead with the rule named.
   - `CampaignRules`: `DailyCap` 10, `Ramp` 3, 5, 10 on the first three days of `on`, five seconds between sends.
   - `RecipientDomainRule`: business domains only (no gmail.com, yahoo.com, outlook.com, hotmail.com, icloud.com and the rest of the list), and MX records checked and found.
   - `Strength`: a formula over stored facts (remote stance, .NET evidence rows, a posted role, the band's top against $155,000 and $170,000, the ghost score, days since first seen, a .NET posting in the last 30 days), the model's step held to plus or minus one, and a 16-digit hash of the inputs so the model is asked again only when a fact moves. A ghost score of 3 or more takes 25 points, more than the rest of the formula spans.
   - `AppliedWhen`: "today", "yesterday", "on Friday", "last Saturday" from AppliedAt and the send time in Central; Track A never past seven days.
   - `ProofKey`: reliability, platform, leadership and ai, each a clause built from a line of the 2026-10-24 resume. No template has a {Proof} line yet; adding one is a template change that waits for Steve.
   - `LinkedInOrder` and `SendOrder`: an accepted invite first, then an invite out more than three days, then the rest; then Strength, then the oldest lead.
   - `TemplateFill`: every {Variable} from the row, a required blank fills nothing and names the blank, and an optional line goes whole with the gap under it when any of its values is blank.

About $4.90 a month, fixed. The free serverless offer was turned down (the review, F2): its allowance is 55 awake hours a month, a campaign that wakes it three times a day spends that by the nineteenth, and then it either pauses with nothing failing loudly or bills by the hour.

Three views for the Sheet mirror later: `LeadsMirror`, `SendsMirror`, `DailyCounts`. CI and the gate compile it to a DACPAC.
   - The Function's identity, `id-jobhunt-ss`, has a contained database user made from its SID, with `db_datareader` and `db_datawriter` on this database only, never on the site's. Made by script on 2026-10-24 (`CREATE USER [id-jobhunt-ss] WITH SID = 0xDD6CF11F4D186148BF294DCB9E7D89C0, TYPE = E`, the SID being the bytes of the identity's client id, the same rule that gives `id-theyard-ss` ADR-039's 0xCAA688281CBEA546A1DEC666B1D193E5) and read back from `sys.database_principals` at 18:38 CDT: `name=id-jobhunt-ss type_desc=EXTERNAL_USER sid_hex=0xDD6CF11F4D186148BF294DCB9E7D89C0`, roles `db_datareader` and `db_datawriter`. The schema was made from these files by the same script and read back: Companies 15 columns, CompanyEvidence 6, Leads 40, EmailsSent 22, three views.

3. **Code renders the approved templates and the Function sends at 8 AM Central, in shadow first.**
   - The templates are Steve's v1 files, byte for byte, compiled into `JobHunt.Application` (`Features/Campaign/Templates/A-v1.md`, `B-v1.md`); `CampaignTemplate.Current` is the one constant a yes to v2 changes, with the v2 files.
   - `TemplateRender` fills every variable from the lead's row through `TemplateFill` and adds nothing: a plain body with the Markdown marks dropped and the links bare, and an HTML body in Gmail's own shape, a div a line, Links in bold and the five links a list of anchors named for what they open, each carrying its `?r=` code. A required blank renders nothing and names it. The renders of both templates, and of an optional line with and without its values, are held byte for byte against checked-in files.
   - `CampaignSend` runs every open lead through the rules in the review's order: former employer, do-not-email, an address with its public source, not bounced, a business domain with MX found, the do-not-apply list and the 30 days, a live thread, Track A within seven days, no blank, WhatTheyDo, no tell in the rendered email. Then it takes the cap (10, or 3 and 5 on the first two days of `on`), the LinkedIn order, Strength and the oldest lead. A lead whose own words fail (a blank, WhatTheyDo, a tell) is marked Blocked for the next lead run; the rest are counted by reason. Version 1 of Template A says "so I applied today" in words, so a Track A email goes out only when the application was today; a yes to v2 lifts that.
   - `CampaignSendTimer` (`CampaignSend` in the Function) fires every hour on the hour in UTC and acts in the 8 o'clock hour Central, which is 13:00 UTC now and 14:00 from November 1, seven days a week. It is idempotent by the day's count in `EmailsSent`. `EMAIL_CAMPAIGN` is `shadow` in the template: every would-send is written with `Shadow = 1` and both bodies, and the mail port is never built; a test holds that. `on` builds `GmailSender`, which sends a multipart message five seconds apart, refuses a header with a line break or a second address, and records the Gmail ids and the Message-Id. One line a pass goes to `campaign/pass.log` on the share, and the run line carries counts only.
   - `CAMPAIGN_SQL` is the connection string with the identity's client id and no secret, set in the template and, for this deploy, with `az functionapp config appsettings set`, which adds and replaces nothing else.

4. **The inbox pass reads what comes back: bounces, replies and requests to stop.**
   - A delivery failure arrives from `mailer-daemon@googlemail.com` in the thread of the email that failed, its plain body naming the address (`Final-Recipient`), the status and a heading such as "Address not found" (read from a real one of 2026-07-28; the test's copy has the address made up). `DeliveryFailure` reads it; the join to `EmailsSent` is the thread and the address together, so a failure for another message in the same thread is not this email's. The email is marked bounced once and the lead's address unverified with the reason.
   - Two failures in one Central day write a row to `campaign/switch.log` on the share: the Function cannot change its own settings, so the trip is a row the send reads before anything else, and while the newest row says `shadow` an `on` campaign writes and does not send. Only a person clears it, with a row of `on` and why. The pass's line says BLOCKED and the run comes to blocked, so the card goes red the hour it happens.
   - Once an hour (the pass at 20 past), each real send of the last 14 days with no reply is read for a sender who is neither Steve nor the mail system. The first such sender marks the email answered; the person waiting is then the reply run's, as any person waiting is. A reply whose own lines, above the quoted original, carry a stop phrase ("not interested", "do not contact", "unsubscribe", "remove me", "no thanks" and four more) marks the company do-not-email with the date, which the send reads before anything but a former employer, and lists the thread in `campaign/stop-threads.log`, which the reply run reads before it drafts (a CONVENTIONS fragment says so).
   - The cost: in shadow nothing was sent, so the part reads nothing. On, at ten a day, at most 140 threads are read once an hour, a metadata read each, about 5 % of the Function's free grant a month at the worst.

5. **The model writes rows to a file; the Function is the one writer of the database.**
   - The lead run is the leads list's run on GitHub Actions (Sonnet, low effort, `LEADS-LANE.md` in the job-hunt folder, rewritten), started by the inbox pass when it harvests a lead nobody has scored; `LEADS_LIST` is `on` from this version. It reads the web for public pages only, with no finder, no sign-in and no guessed address, and it posts its usage under "Azure Functions, leads lane".
   - The run has no identity the database knows, so it writes one file to `campaign/leads-inbox/` on the share, and `LeadImport` reads it in the inbox pass: every company and lead held to `LeadSpool`'s rules (a registrable key, a named manager, Track A only with its application, an address only with its https page and never a personal one, a known proof key, links that carry the lead's code), a refused row named in `import.log` and written nowhere, a WhatTheyDo that fails kept with Blocked so the next run sees why, each new address asked for its MX records of a public resolver (`DohMxCheck`, dns.google, keyless), and Strength and its inputs hash computed by code. The file moves to merged/ or, unreadable, to rejected/.

## Where it sits

A new slice, `Campaign`, in the job hunt's Function rings, beside `Leads`, whose Rule 50 name match it reuses rather than copies. Separation of concerns is the principle: the model's judgement stays in rows a person can read and a rule can refuse, and the send is code a test can run. It cost one folder of rules and a copied Python file in the tests. What would change it: Steve approving a finder, a template with a {Proof} line, or a different cap, each a change to one constant or one adapter with its test.

## Addendum, 2026-10-26 (1.0.4.89): a date goes to SQL as a date

The first morning with `EMAIL_CAMPAIGN` on would have failed before it sent anything. To count the day of `on` for the ramp, the send asked the email log for every row since `DateOnly.MinValue`, the year 1. The store turned that day into a `DateTime` and handed it to `AddWithValue`, and Microsoft.Data.SqlClient sends a `DateTime` it was not told the type of as SQL's old `datetime`, whose range starts in 1753. The client refused it before the query left the Function ("SqlDateTime overflow"). Shadow never reached the line, because shadow has no day of `on`, so every shadow morning passed and no test saw it: the test's memory log accepted any day.

From this version:

- **The day count has its own question.** The email log answers the Central days that carry a real send (`OnDaysAsync`: the distinct `CentralDay` of every row with `Shadow = 0`), and the send counts the ones before today. No query asks for "everything since the beginning of time".
- **A date to SQL is `SqlDbType.Date`.** The store passes a `DateOnly` and its one command builder adds it as a `date` parameter, so a date never travels as `datetime` and never meets the 1753 floor. The columns it is written to (`CentralDay`, `FirstSeen`, `LastDotNetPostingAt`) are `date` already.
- **The test's log holds SQL's range.** The memory log the send tests use refuses a day before 1753 the way the client does, so a path that asks for one fails in the test and not on the first real morning. A test drives the `on` path with one real send on an earlier day and reads the second day's cap of five.

`CampaignSqlTests.A_date_goes_to_SQL_as_a_date` and `CampaignSendTests.The_on_path_counts_its_days_with_dates_SQL_accepts` hold it.

## Addendum, 2026-10-26 (1.0.4.90): one slow board does not stop the morning

The board scan runs first in the 8 o'clock hour, one board after another, and the send waits for it. A board that did not answer was meant to be skipped, but only a network error was treated that way. A board that held the request past HttpClient's 100 seconds surfaces as a cancellation, and a board that answered with a page of HTML instead of its JSON surfaces as a parse error; either one escaped the scan and failed the whole pass before the send, so that morning would have sent nothing and written no line.

From this version:

- **Each public request has fifteen seconds.** The board reader and the MX check each wait at most fifteen seconds a request, and treat a timeout, a body that is not JSON and JSON of the wrong shape the same way as a network error: the service did not answer, the port says null, and the scan moves to the next board. A cancellation the host asks for, when the Function is stopping, still goes on up.
- **The scan cannot stop the send.** The timer runs the scan inside its own guard. A scan that throws anyway is logged as an error and its part of `campaign/pass.log` reads `scan: FAILED: <the exception's type>`, and the send runs on the postings already in the book.
- **The pass line says how long the scan took**, in whole seconds, so a scan creeping toward the host's ten-minute limit shows before it reaches it.

`LeadImportTests.A_board_that_does_not_answer_in_time_or_answers_with_a_page_is_not_answered`, `LeadImportTests.A_resolver_that_does_not_answer_in_time_or_answers_with_a_page_is_not_known` and `LeadImportTests.A_scan_that_throws_is_written_as_failed_and_the_send_still_runs` hold it.

## Addendum, 2026-10-27 (1.0.4.106): the company mailbox is declared, never inferred from a missing name
