# ADR: The nightly picker, code gates and the model ranks

Status: accepted, 2026-11-05, at 1.0.4.143, its jobs mode in nightly.yml at 1.0.4.144, after the two versions before it (1.0.4.141 and 1.0.4.142) that cleaned the alert leads and kept every posting the board scan reads. The job hunt's Function picks the night's roles in the 2 o'clock hour Central with every rule code can decide, and a Claude lane on a GitHub runner ranks what is left and writes the morning's file.

## In plain words

Every morning should start with a list of real jobs that were checked overnight, so the day goes to applying and not to searching. Rules that never change, such as remote only, posted in the last three weeks, the right level, the right stack, the pay floor, not applied before, and no more than three applications to one company in two months, are code with tests. The parts that need reading and weighing, such as whether the posting is still open on the employer's own site, how it ranks, and whether its hiring process is a wall of automated tests, are done by a model that writes a file. Nothing in the night submits, sends or signs in anywhere.

What that is worth: a reviewer can see exactly which roles were thrown away and why, because every role a gate refused is written with the gate's name, and the model can only choose among roles code already let through.

## Context

On 5 November there was no list waiting. Three night pieces existed and none produced one: the inbox pass harvested alert leads and threw away real ones (fixed in 1.0.4.141), the board scan ran at 8 AM after Steve was awake and kept one date a company (moved to 1 AM and made to keep every posting in 1.0.4.142), and the postings history had been planned and not built. Steve's ask, 5 November: ten to twenty roles a night, ready to read in for blockers.

## Decision

1. **The gates are code in the Function.** `JobsGates` (Domain, Jobs slice) holds every deterministic rule with named cases: not a staffing firm placing an unnamed client and not a former employer; remote, or St. Louis hybrid at one day a week at most, with a St. Louis hybrid of unknown days passed on to Steve's table; posted or first seen 21 days ago or less; Principal, Staff, Lead, Architect, an engineering Manager or Director, and a plain Senior only with a band topping $180,000; a band whose top is under $170,000 skipped with its number; a stack that touches C#, .NET or Azure or an AI-native lead role; not the same role applied to; and under RULE 50's three applications a company in 60 days. The first failure is written with its gate.
2. **One pay line, in one place.** `PayFloor` (Domain, Shared/Pay) holds RULE 72's $180,000 pass and $170,000 flag. The picker and the campaign's `Strength` read it, so the band points of the email campaign moved with it (Strength's formula version 2).
3. **The picker runs in the clock's own hour, not a new slot.** `Nightly` asks `CentralClock.IsJobsHour` (the 2 o'clock hour Central, asked as a local hour) and runs `JobsPick` through `JobsHour` before it dispatches `jobs` on the nightly switch. NightlySlots is not touched, because the Admin card judges a month of firings against that table.
4. **The picker reads, and writes one file.** `JobsPick` (Application) reads the postings the 1 AM scan saw open tonight with their company's name, a description only for a posting the other gates passed, today's and yesterday's alert leads that passed the inbox pass, the tracker's SQL copy and the do-not-apply list, and writes `nightly/jobs-candidates-<date>.tsv`: the supply line, one row a candidate, and every refused role under `# FAILED` with its gate. An alert lead whose company and title the scan read tonight is dropped for the posting, which is confirmed. Without the postings tables it writes from the alert leads alone and says so; with no tracker copy it reads the share's CSV and says so.
5. **The model ranks and writes the morning's file.** `nightly.yml` gains the `jobs` mode on Opus with the web, 80 turns and 40 minutes, reading `nightly/JOBS-LANE.md` on the share: confirm each alert lead on the employer's own board, read wage filings where no band was published, rank, scan for expectations, evergreen postings and blind-test funnels, and write `JOBS-<date>.md` with its READY TO APPLY table in the columns the 6 AM apply lane parses, its NEEDS STEVE, UNCONFIRMED, FLAG and SKIPPED tables and its supply line, then append the ready rows to PRIORITY-BOARD.md. The lane submits nothing, sends nothing and signs in nowhere.

## What holds it

`JobsTests` (every gate, the pay lines, the alert rows, the candidates file's shape, the drop of an alert the scan confirmed, the two fall-backs, the hour and the statements' columns; the jobs mode in nightly.yml from 1.0.4.144), `CampaignTextTests` (Strength on the new lines), `PostingsTests` and `PostingsSeedTests` (the postings the picker reads).

## Not done in this version, and why

- **The picker's card.** Every job-hunt process gets its own card under Cloud infrastructure; the Admin slice belongs to another lane this week, which reads the picker's facts (its run name, its hour, its file and its counts) from that lane's DONE note.
- **Confirmation from the cloud for every board.** Ashby and some Workday tenants serve JavaScript only, so the lane cannot read them from a runner; those rows are written as unconfirmed for the daytime lane.

## Where it sits

A feature slice, Jobs, in each of the Function's three rings, a shared Pay concern beside the shared level rule, and one mode of the GitHub workflow that already runs the job hunt's lanes. Separation of duties is the principle: code holds every rule that can be written down, the model reads and ranks, and a person or the 6 AM program applies in the morning. It cost one hour of the clock's day and one model run a night. What would change it: a board the runner cannot read becoming the norm, which would move confirmation to the box's own browser lane, a new record.

## Addendum, 2026-11-07 (1.0.4.158): the picker has a card

The picker and the board scan before it get a card under Cloud infrastructure, after WickedAwsome. It reads what the Function already writes: the Nightly function's "jobs:" line in the 2 o'clock hour (the candidates file, its supply line and the dispatch) and the BoardScan function's "board scan:" line at 1 AM, each joined on its operation to the request the Functions host wrote for that firing, from the Log Analytics workspace with the site's own Log Analytics Reader role, the way the run histories are read. The share's own files (the pick log and the candidates file) are not read: the site holds no data role on the share, and adding one is not this card's to do. The facts change once a night, so the card is read once an hour, not every minute. Its rows ask whether the pick and the scan ran (red when a run failed, amber past a day and an hour), what the picker read (amber while postings are blocked, with the cause named), and what it costs; GB-seconds say "not read", because the firing's execution units are not split out of the Function's. Counts only: never a company, a title or a lead. PickerCardTests holds it.

## Addendum, 2026-11-10 (1.0.5.15): two remote reads both paths share

Measured on the night of 10 November: two of the seven roles the 2 o'clock lane put on READY were not remote for Steve, and both were queued for a note. One posting's location was "United States" and its text asked a hire to reside in one of nineteen named states, Missouri not among them; the other's location was "United States" and its text never said remote. `RemoteRead` answered "remote" for both, because a location that is only a country counted as remote. Two reads change in `JobsGates`, so the 2 o'clock pick and the pounce below both get them:

1. **Named states.** When the text names five or more US states in one run, with a cue in the 200 characters before it (reside, located, eligible, following states, hire in, work from, based in, live in), and Missouri is not one of them, the role fails as `remote in named states, Missouri not among them:` and the codes. A code may carry a footnote star, as the board's own text writes "*IL". A list that includes Missouri is not a rule. Neither is a list of four or fewer, nor one with no cue before it (offices, customers).
2. **Country only.** When the stance is not Remote, the location is only a country (United States, USA, US), the text has been read, and neither title nor text says remote, the read is `country only, remote unsaid`. The night lets it through as a flag, exactly as it treats `hybrid, days unknown`, so the 2 o'clock lane must confirm it. On a first pass with no text the read stays remote, so a description is still asked for.

Every other gate is unchanged. A dry pass over the 31 postings the first pass kept on 10 November (23:37 CST) passed 17 before the change and 16 after: the country-only posting moved from a pass to a flag, the named-states postings now fail on their states (two of them failed on their stack before), and nothing else moved. `PounceRulesTests` holds both reads on the two postings' own text.

## Addendum, 2026-11-10 (1.0.5.15): the pounce, a stricter gate on every new posting and the clock it starts

Steve's measure, 10 November: minutes from a posting going live to the first message reaching a hiring manager or VP, under 60, every hour of the day, each role's clock kept in SQL. The 2 o'clock pick reads a posting once a night, so a posting that went up at 10 AM waited sixteen hours for its first read. From this version the Function judges each new posting within minutes of the fresh pass finding it (ADR-118's addendum of 1.0.5.11) and starts a clock on every role that passes. This would sit in a record of its own; a new record file needs a line in the site's documentation catalogue, which this version could not touch, so it is written here, beside the picker whose gates it reuses.

1. **The tick.** `PounceTick` (Application, Jobs slice), timer `Pounce` at five past each quarter hour, five minutes behind the fresh pass, switch `POUNCE` (only "off" stops it). Each posting first seen in the last two hours with no row in `jobhunt.PounceRoles` is judged. It gets a row whether it passed or not. It sends nothing and touches no one.
2. **The pounce gate, stricter than the night's,** because nothing reads a role after it: `JobsGates.Judge` with the posting's text, then the remote read exactly `remote`, then a seat his targeting file names (`PounceRules.IsSeat`: software engineering, architecture and engineering management in; customer-facing implementation, product, sales, marketing, design, data science, security and the rest out), then a company not blocked whole by BLACKLIST.md (a row whose Person cell opens "ALL OF", and the names it says it includes; a row for one person or one job blocks no company), then no role passed in 30 days with the same company, base title and level (the picker's key, now `JobsGates.Same`). The first refusal is written. The seat lists bind the pounce only; the 2 o'clock pick does not read them. On the dry pass above the pounce passed 3 of the 31.
3. **Where each instant comes from.** `LiveAt` is the board's instant (`Postings.PublishedAt`) when it gives one no later than `FirstSeenAt`, else `FirstSeenAt` with `LiveKnown` 0. A touch is a tracker row at the same company (the company cell up to " (", matched by RULE 50's matcher), written to SQL at or after `LiveAt` and dated no earlier than its Central day, that reached a person (`TrackerRows.Channel`, moved to Shared from the funnel line so both read one rule) and names a leader seat in Person, Company or the first 300 characters of Notes; or a `campaign.EmailsSent` row with `Shadow` 0 for that company, sent at or after `LiveAt`, to an address carrying the contact's own name. `FirstTouchAt` is the earliest, the tracker's `WrittenAt` or the email's `SentAt`, set once and never moved.
4. **The lines.** `process:Pounce` a tick (detail the Central day; `fresh`, `passed`, `dropped`, `touched`, `waiting`, `late`), one line a tick on the share's `nightly/pounce.log` naming each passed role's company, title, live and seen times, and on the :05 tick of each hour and any tick that passed a role or found a touch, `process:PounceDay` for each of the last seven Central days (`caught`, `touched`, `median_min`, `worst_min`, `waiting`, `oldest_wait_min`), the contract the Admin card reads. `POST /api/pounce/hand-run` with the function key runs one tick with its day lines.
5. **Light on Basic.** The whole tracker is read only on a tick with a fresh posting (RULE 50 and applied-already need it); the touch check reads only the rows written since the oldest open role went live; a quiet tick reads neither.

**What it cannot see.** The tracker holds no hour: `WrittenAt` is when a row reached SQL, minutes after the send, and a row the merger rewrites (a fix, or the positions after a moved-out row) gets a new `WrittenAt`, which the Date guard keeps from reading as a new touch only when the row's day is older than the live day. A touch the tracker never records, a reply by phone, a message sent from another account, is not seen. A board that gives no instant starts the clock at the first sighting, up to fifteen minutes late, and `LiveKnown` says so. 98 of the campaign's 99 sends went to a shared mailbox under a leader's name; the named-address rule counts none of those as a touch, so the clock will read long until the campaign reaches people by name. `PounceRulesTests` and `PounceTickTests` hold it.
